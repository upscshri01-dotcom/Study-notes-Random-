# Over 1.28 lakh Form 7s filed just before Karnataka SIR; 45,878 by third parties

**Third-party applications comprise 35.6% of deletion requests made between the announcement of SIR and the freezing of rolls; in at least 28 Assembly constituencies, more than 1,000 Form 7 applications were filed before enumeration began**

Rishitha Khanna, Pon Vasanth B.A.
BENGALURU/CHENNAI

In the month just before the special intensive revision (SIR) of electoral rolls began across Karnataka, more than 1.28 lakh Form 7 applications seeking deletion of names from the rolls were filed, including 45,878 by third parties — people other than the electors concerned or election officials, The Hindu has found.

Of the 45,878 third-party applications, 2,254 were “bulk applications” filed by just 112 objectors, each of whom submitted at least 11 or more Form 7 applications.

Basamma from Sindhanur filed 96 applications, the highest during the pre-SIR period (84 against electors in the same polling booth). Hanamant Calavadi from Bagalkot filed 62 applications, all targeting a single polling station.

The third-party applications accounted for 35.6% of all deletion requests received between the announcement of the exercise on May 14 and the freezing of the rolls on June 16 (to establish a baseline for the revision). Of the 45,878 applications, 36,439 cited death as the reason for seeking deletion, while 6,775 mentioned “absent by permanent shift”.

In at least 28 Assembly constituencies, more than 1,000 Form 7 applications were filed before enumeration began. Of these, 20 are represented by the Congress. More than 28,441 of the total applications were concentrated in just four constituencies — Bagalkot, Belagavi North, Sindhanur and Nippani. Barring Nippani, the other three are represented by the Congress.

District-wise, Belagavi reported the highest number of applications, at 38,713, followed by Bagalkot with 13,889 and Raichur with 10,454.

All three are Congress-dominated districts, with Raichur and Bagalkot also among the State’s backward districts.

The Hindu recently reported on Belagavi North, where BJP workers distributed pre-filled Form 7 applications targeting Muslim electors to Booth Level Officers (BLOs) in at least six polling stations.

The 1.28 lakh-plus Form 7 applications were filed before BLOs began distributing enumeration forms on June 30 as part of the SIR. This meant that deletion requests were made even before the electors’ details could be verified through the enumeration process.

In the week before the rolls were frozen, between June 10 and June 16 alone, 20,671 Form 7 applications were submitted. On May 14, the day the SIR was announced in Karnataka, 3,624 applications were filed. However, it remains unclear why Form 7 applications were accepted before the SIR began, when the enumeration phase was intended to identify the same categories of electors for whom Form 7 can be used to seek deletion. Form 7 can be filed to seek deletion of a name on grounds including death, permanent shifting, duplicates, underage enrolment or non-citizenship.

During the SIR, BLOs were to distribute enumeration forms to electors and identify those who were Absent, Shifted, Dead, Duplicate and Others (ASDD) — the very categories on which Form 7 applications can seek deletions. At the draft-roll stage, 1.07 crore names had been excluded under these categories in the State.

Multiple Assistant Electoral Registration Officers (AEROs) confirmed to The Hindu that Form 7 applications had been accepted before enumeration began and processed after the mandated one-week period.

When asked why Form 7 applications were accepted before the SIR, Karnataka Chief Electoral Officer (CEO) V. Anbu Kumar said, “The pending forms were processed till June 16, 2026, and on the basis of records Form 7s were accepted.” He did not clarify whether the names targeted by these applications had been deleted from the existing rolls before enumeration began or whether the electors concerned had been served notices.

# Congress names 208 observers for anti-SIR campaign

The Hindu Bureau
NEW DELHI

The Congress has appointed 208 party leaders as observers to coordinate and monitor its nationwide Vote Bachao, Desh Bachao (save vote, save country) campaign against the special intensive revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, signalling its intent to sustain the agitation against alleged vote theft.

The appointments, approved by Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge, were announced on Friday night by general secretary (organisation) K.C. Venugopal.

The list includes senior leaders such as Adhir Ranjan Chowdhury, Avinash Pande, Prithviraj Chavan, Nana Patole, K.S. Suresh, Ashok Tanwar and Ajoy Kumar, among others.

For organisational gaps
The observers will work with State units to oversee the ongoing campaign, which began on October 10, across clusters of districts, strengthen booth-level mobilisation and address organisational gaps.

The announcement follows a series of programmes announced by the party earlier this month to challenge the SIR exercise and press its demand for the removal of Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar.

At a meeting chaired by Mr. Kharge on October 5, attended by Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha Rahul Gandhi, the Congress had announced plans to reach out to the more than 13.3 crore voters whose names have been deleted from electoral rolls. Booth-level agents, designated as vote rakshaks (vote guardians) have been tasked with identifying voters whose names have been unfairly deleted and help them enroll.

The campaign also envisaged block-level marches, village meetings and mobilisation at the grass-roots to raise awareness about voting rights. The party has maintained that the issue goes beyond electoral politics and concerns citizens’ constitutional right to vote.

The latest appointments are intended to strengthen coordination between the party’s central leadership and its State and district units as the campaign expands.

Shiv Sahay Singh
KOLKATA

West Bengal Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) president and Rajya Sabha MP Samik Bhattacharya on Saturday called for a fresh special intensive revision (SIR) of the electoral rolls in the State, amidst protests rages across the country demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar and scrapping of the SIR exercise.

There is a need to have SIR once again here, because SIR was not properly carried out here. # Imran Khan moves HC against ‘illegal’ notice to leave India

Vinaya Deshpande Pandit
MUMBAI

Terming the “leave India notice” issued by him the Foreigners Regional Registration Office (FRRO) in Mumbai as arbitrary, illegal, and unconstitutional, actor Imran Khan on Saturday moved the Bombay High Court seeking to get it quashed.

The actor’s legal team is likely to mention the matter in the Bombay High Court on Monday.

Meanwhile, the FRRO on Saturday issued another notice to the actor, a US citizen who has an Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) card, asking him to explain why his OCI card should not be cancelled. Mr. Khan has been given 15 days to respond. The earlier notice had asked the actor to leave the country before October 28.

‘Notice is violation’
In his writ petition filed on Saturday, Mr. Khan said that the notice had led to the violation of Sections 14 and 21 of the Constitution. He claimed that the action against him followed a politically motivated complaint and asked why due process was not followed in the matter.

The actor has also sought to know the basis on which the notice was issued. “The LIN [leave India notice] directs the petitioner to leave India by 28.10.2026 without disclosing any reasons... It was issued without inquiry, a show-cause notice, or any opportunity for the petitioner to respond to the alleged violations,” the writ gear tendered.

On October 10, Mr. Khan participated in a protest held in Mumbai seeking the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. He was later detained.

# SC to hear plea against preventive detention

Krishnadas Rajagopal
NEW DELHI

The Supreme Court has agreed to hear academician and columnist Apporvanad Jha, who has submitted that equating the right to protest with a breach of peace is unwarranted and a ploy by the Central government and the Delhi Police to make preventive detentions.

The written submissions were filed additionally as part of a criminal writ petition moved by Mr. Jha, represented by senior advocate Nitya Ramakrishnan and advocate Akriti Chaubey, which challenged the legality of prohibitory orders issued by the authorities under Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS). The Bench has agreed to list the writ petition for hearing on November 19.

If every call for a peaceful protest is considered a breach of peace by the authorities, no citizen can ever exercise the right of public demonstration, Mr. Jha contended. In additional submissions, detailing specific instances, the submissions said these detentions happened during the protests against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar.

The academician said the modus operandi of the Central government, in conjunction with the Delhi Police, seems to be to first refuse permission to hold a demonstration, and then, by covering practically New and Central Delhi with prohibitory orders, to force an “illegality” or a violation of an order of a police officer and make preventive detentions.

The petitioner has urged the court to call for records from police stations and detention centres, and seek an explanation on these “misdeeds and direct action” against police personnel. He submitted that the police were liable to pay compensation for these “gross violations of rules”.

# 10 FIRs registered against right-wing activists over assaults on protesters

Lavpreet Kaur
NEW DELHI

Taking cognisance of a slew of social media posts, the Delhi Police registered at least 10 FIRs for allegedly assaulting protesters during the ongoing demonstrations in the national capital demanding roll back of the special intensive revision (SIR) and the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. Most of the social media posts are about right-wing activists filed against right-wing activists.

This comes a week after citizens, activists and media organisations shared video clips and photos across social media platforms, tagging Delhi Police and demanding action against alleged assaults, most of whom were identified as right-wing activists, accused of attacking protesters.

The social media links and screenshots showing assaults, including those shared by Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), Indian Youth Congress, along with other political parties and activists, have been attached in the document seen by The Hindu.

“We have taken cognisance of the posts and registered FIRs. In some cases, the accused were arrested, while other accused involved in violence are being identified and traced,” said a Delhi Police official. “We urge citizens to attach Delhi Police in posts about such assaults so we can continue taking action,” said the official.

According to details shared by the police, four people identified as Raman Saini, 50, a resident of Pahari Dheeraj in Delhi; Vivekanand, 32, from Nangloi; Kapil Kumar, 27, who lives in Ghaffarabad, Delhi, Pradesh; and Ravinder Singh alias Govind Hindu, 35, from Indirapuram in Ghaziabad, were arrested after a video showing individuals assaulting a male protester and a female woman during the October 10 protest. The case was registered at Kamla Market police station on October 11 under Sections 115(2), 126 (2) and 3(3) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS).

Hindu Raksha Dal president Pinky Chaudhary subsequently claimed that the accused were members of his organisation and vowed to protect them.

# Teja Singh Samundri’s legacy drives BJP’s pro-Sikh push in Punjab

Vikas Vasudeva
CHANDIGARH

With the 2027 Punjab Assembly election nearing, the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) is trying to build a pro-Sikh image by weaving historical Sikh narratives, and Sardar Teja Singh Samundri, a key architect of the Sikh gurdwara reform movement, and a co-founder of the Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee (SGPC), has become one of the focal points of that outreach.

As the centenary of his martyrdom is being observed this year, the top leadership, including Prime Minister Narendra Modi, have frequently invoked Samundri’s legacy at public rallies. Samundri, who refused to compromise on his principles and declined conditional release from British custody, died in 1926 at the age of 43 at the Lahore Central Jail.

The Sikh icon actively participated in several agitations, including the Chaba Morcha (in 1921-22 for the control the keys to the treasury of the Golden Temple in Amritsar); the Guru Ka Bagh Morcha (in 1922 for the control the keys to the Guru ka Bagh gurdwara in Ajnala); and the Jaito da Morcha (in 1923, over the demand of Maharaja Ripudaman Singh of Nabha by the British).

Samundri was arrested several times.

The naming of the Teja Singh Samundri Hall at the Golden Temple complex in Amritsar serves as a reminder of his pivotal role in shaping modern Sikhism.

The Central government’s Azadi Ka Amrit Mahotsav initiative pays tribute to Samundri’s contributions. By evoking Samundri’s legacy, the BJP wants to place sikh sacrifice within the larger story of the freedom struggle and the party’s focus on nationalism.

Pro-Sikh makeover
The BJP has tried to draw attention to its pro-Sikh image by highlighting the steps taken by the Union government since 2014, including the formation of a Special Investigation Team to probe the 1984 anti-Sikh riots; the opening of the Kartarpur Corridor, which connects the Kartarpur sahib gurdwara in Pakistan with Dera Baba Nanak shrine in India’s Gurdaspur district in Punjab; and granting FCRA (Foreign Contribution Regulation Act) registration for Harmandir Sahib.

The BJP has also declared December 26 as “Veer Bal Diwas” to mark the martyrdom of Sahibzada Zorawar Singh and Baba Fateh Singh, sons of Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth Sikh Guru. The appointment of Kewal Singh Dhillon, a Jat Sikh, and his appointment as the party’s new State president is seen as another attempt by the party to consolidate its position in the community.

“The BJP is trying to cultivate many Sikhs as possible,” Amarjit Singh Narang, former Professor of Political Science, Indira Gandhi National Open University, told The Hindu.

Idea of BJP-SAD alliance
“There is speculation about a possible alliance between the BJP and the Shiromani Akali Dal (SAD). While that may happen or not, these efforts by the BJP to woo Sikhs could favor the BJP either way. If they win over Sikhs with an alliance, it is good enough for them. If an alliance with the SAD does materialise, that would also be beneficial for the BJP as there are no ideological differences. The original SAD-BJP partnership was built on Hindu-Sikh unity, which remained a cornerstone of their alliance for decades,” Prof. Narang said.

Professor Karamjeet Singh, Vice-Chancellor of Guru Nanak Dev University, who recently translated into English a book on Samundri by Piara Singh, said Samundri’s legacy is as relevant in the modern world as it was years ago. “His courage, spiritual depth and commitment to the community is a path that has left an indelible mark on Sikh society and the Indian national movement. His journey from brief military service in the British Indian Army to becoming a reformer, educator and martyr reflects a life anchored in equality, faith and resistance against oppression,” Prof. Karamjeet Singh said.

Professor Manjit Singh, former head of the Department of Sociology, Panjab University, Chandigarh, said the BJP had faced backlash earlier, particularly the traditionally agrarian Jat Sikh community in Punjab, over the controversial farm laws. “The party’s invasion of Sikh personalities appears to be a strategy to neutralise that damage. They have really been provoking into prominent Sikh personalities, because it be Maharaja Ranjit Singh or Teja Singh Samundri,” Prof. Manjit Singh said.
