17 killed, 25 injured in Kolkata puja stampede
West Bengal CM says a misleading announcement by organisers of a puja pandal in north Kolkata triggered the stampede; organisers detained and venue closed; an investigation is under way

Shrabana Chatterjee
Shiv Sahay Singh
KOLKATA

At least 17 people, most of them women and children, died and 25 others were injured after a stampede broke out at a Durga Puja pandal in Kolkata on Saturday night. The incident took place around 7.40 p.m. at the exit gate of the pandal, a statement from the police said.

According to reports, a large crowd had gathered at the Chorbagan Sarbojanin Durgostab in Kolkata’s Girish Park area and tried to take shelter inside the pandal after a brief spell of rainfall. Firefighters, disaster management team, and senior police officials rushed to the spot for rescue operations, according to a police statement.

Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari said that the stampede broke out after a misleading announcement was made by the organisers of the Durga Puja pandal along with the blowing of some whistles. He said that the organisers have been detained at the local police station and an investigation will be held to ascertain the reasons behind the stampede.

"This is a painful incident. 17 persons have died. We stand by the family of the dead," Mr. Adhikari said. The injured have been admitted to a State-run health facility. "Our priority is to give medical aid to the injured. Of the 25 injured, one is critical, and others are stable," Mr. Adhikari said. The Chief Minister said that he was at Medical College, Kolkata, and had also spoken to the family of the deceased.

The Durga Puja pandal was closed after the stampede. After the incident, local people gave a hand to rescue the injured. Eyewitnesses said that there was not much police presence at the pandal when the stampede broke out. The pandal is located in a narrow area, and access to it was blocked by stalls that have come up around it during the festival season.

Dipke, Neha released as protests continue

Lavpreet Kaur
NEW DELHI

Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) founder Abhijeet Dipke, who was placed under preventive arrest by the Delhi Police, was released on bail by a Delhi court on Saturday after signing a peace bond, even as sporadic protests, led by women activists, continued for the eighth day against the Chief Election Commissioner and police action. AISA president Neha Bora was also released after being kept in detention for around 12 hours.

Mr. Dipke was detained on Friday ahead of the ‘Lehra Do Tiranga’ march. A Special Executive Magistrate ordered his release after he signed a ₹50,000 bond stating that he would not participate in any activity that would breach peace or create a law-and-order situation.

Around 150 people took part in two protest marches in the capital. Women representatives from the All India Democratic Women’s Association, the National Federation of Indian Women, the All India Progressive Women’s Association, the All India Mahila Sanskritik Sangathan, the All India Feminist Alliance, and other groups took out a march along Ashoka Road to the Delhi Police headquarters against the alleged “sexualised and gender-based violence” by Delhi Police personnel.

Children have fundamental right to access Internet: SC

Krishnadas Rajagopal
NEW DELHI

The Supreme Court has held that children, like all individuals, have a fundamental right to access and use the Internet under Articles 21 and 19(1)(a) of the Constitution.

A Bench of Justices J.B. Pardiwala and Vinod K. Chandran, in its order, cautioned that blanket bans on children’s online access were “unproven measures of proven disproportionality”. It said the safety of children and their freedom were not at odds with each other and that an atmosphere of hostility and stringent restrictions could stop children, enticed or threatened online, from reporting such incidents to parents or guardians for fear of reprisal for using the Internet.
