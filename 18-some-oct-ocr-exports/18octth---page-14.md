### Trump waives sanction over Russian oil; India gets relief
The waiver assures Indian refiners of greater flexibility to continue purchasing oil from Moscow; the move comes just ahead of U.S. Secretary of State Rubio's visit to India during Oct. 20 to 22

Saptaparno Ghosh
Suhasini Haidar
NEW DELHI
U.S. President Donald Trump on Saturday waived key provisions of a law that authorised punitive tariffs on countries purchasing Russian oil, offering relief to India.

The 'Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026' (SRIA Act), ratified on September 18, authorised increasing tariffs on countries continuing to buy Russian oil by as much as 100% as Washington sought to tighten Moscow's oil revenues.

The waiver announced on Saturday assures Indian refiners of greater flexibility to continue purchasing oil from Moscow, with supplies from West Asia affected because of the resurgence of conflict.

The decision by Mr. Trump that comes just ahead of the October 18 deadline set by the Congress for a determination on sanctions is also expected to ease the discussions during U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio's visit to Mumbai and Jamnagar from October 20 to 22, when he will meet External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar.

Days after the legislation was signed into law, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman had also said that India could not stop buying Russian oil, as doing so could raise global oil prices and alternative sources were limited amid the conflict in the Gulf.

The availability of Russian oil — now without any threat of sanctions — could ease the supply situation in the global oil market, which has been facing a crunch because of the renewed West Asia conflict.

### Militarisation persists in Sri Lanka's Tamil areas, says rights commission
Meera Srinivasan
COLOMBO
Militarisation of Sri Lanka's Tamil-majority north and east persists 17 years after the civil war, despite many state assurances to reduce the military footprint in the former conflict zone, the Human Rights Commission of Sri Lanka has said. In a recent report titled "The Human Rights Situation of the Northern and Eastern Provinces of Sri Lanka", the commission pointed to a "sustained pattern of land appropriation, denial of access to traditional livelihoods, and intimidation of local populations" by the military in the districts across the region.

"Seventeen years after the end of the armed conflict, militarisation remains a persistent feature of governance and land use in the Northern and Eastern Provinces," the report said, pointing to "repeated assurances" by the state to reduce the military footprint and return occupied lands to their rightful owners.

The Commission urged the government to expedite the return of military-occupied lands to the people and ensure full restoration of property rights and livelihoods. Further, it emphasised that the government refrain from invoking national security "as a blanket justification for land acquisition", particularly where such actions disproportionately affect minority communities or facilitate religious encroachment, and "take concrete steps" to withdraw military involvement from civilian economic sectors, especially agriculture and education.

The Human Rights Commission urged the government to expedite the return of military-occupied lands to the people.

Militarisation is one of the prominent post-war concerns highlighted in the Commission's report, which looks at wide-ranging issues such as enforced disappearances, mass graves, memorialisation, state surveillance and intimidation, land contestations, resettlement of northern Muslims expelled by the LTTE in 1990, freedom of religion, development projects, and livelihoods.

Residents of the north and east have consistently cited military presence and engagement as an impediment to post-war reconciliation and economic revival. Families have been demanding the return of lands, where their homes once stood, or they once farmed.

Election promise
Return of people's lands was among the chief promises made by President Anura Kumara Dissanayake to the Tamil people, ahead of the November 2024 general elections. His National People's Power emerged winner in all but one of the five electoral districts in the north and east. In August this year, Mr. Dissanayake, who is also the Minister of Defence, instructed the security forces to expedite the lease and return of land taken over during the war and no longer required for security purposes. However, despite his directives to the military, Tamil-speaking people living in the north and east are concerned about the slow pace of action.

When contacted, Ministry spokesman Brig. Franklin Joseph claimed "over 90%" of the lands held previously by the military have been returned to the people. "There are many cases where the owners are not known or there is no proof confirming their ownership. Those are being looked into."

Vikaran, MP from the opposition Illankai Tamil Arasu Kadchi (ITAK) representing the northern Mullaitivu district, contended that it was unfair to expect families who fled the district at the height of the war to produce documents. "Our people ran for their lives... in that sort of life and death situation, you cannot expect them to carry all their documents in files," he told The Hindu.

"I can tell you that huge swathes of people's land remain under military control even today. Not just the military, but also other state agencies like the Archaeology and Wildlife departments have been resorting to blatant land grabs. That is our reality," he said, highlighting an enduring struggle for people of Mullaitivu, the district that witnessed the final battle between the armed forces and the LTTE. It was also where the army is accused of killing tens and thousands of civilians, including in areas declared no fire zones. The military and successive governments in Colombo have denied the allegations.

"When I say military, it is the army, navy, air force, civil defence force, all of them who control land," added the legislator.

The report pointed to military involvement in economic activities, such as agriculture and pre-school teaching, predominantly executed through the Civil Security Department. Military officials are administration of teachers in pre-schools," it noted. In Mullaitivu district, it is not uncommon to see armed military men in uniform, running shops or manning traffic at junctions.

Observing that Sri Lanka "currently lacks a comprehensive national security sector reform plan" that includes strategies to reduce military presence in the North and East and restore civilian administration in the region, the Commission noted that "continued presence of the military in multiple aspects of civilian life singles this region out from the rest of the country and inhibits progress on post-war reconciliation".

### Oil tankers on fire after hitting mines in Hormuz: Iran Guards
Agence France-Presse
TEHRAN
Iran's Revolutionary Guards on Saturday said two oil tankers were on fire in the Strait of Hormuz after hitting mines in the waterway at the heart of the West Asia war.

"A supertanker in violation of regulations, carrying more than two million barrels of crude oil and attempting to pass illegally through the Strait of Hormuz, struck a mine, triggering an explosion and a fierce fire," the Revolutionary Guards said.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards say each tanker carries more than two million barrels of crude oil

The statement did not specify the exact location of the incident.

Later on Saturday, the Guards said "a second offending supertanker was engulfed in a massive blaze after hitting a naval mine," adding it also carried "more than two million barrels of crude oil" and was attempting to pass the Strait. The origins of the load and the tanker's flag were not immediately disclosed.

"The tanker attempted to cross the strait with U.S. support... by deactivating all its tracking systems," the Guards added.

On Thursday and Friday, Iran's Army said two tankers had caught fire in what Tehran calls its "pro-hibited zone" in the Gulf of Oman.

Also on Friday, maritime monitoring agencies reported that two tankers had been struck in Gulf waters of Saudi Arabia's coast.

### Williams and Perovic earn the bragging rights for BFC
ISL
Sports Bureau
Ryan Williams and Mihailo Perovic netted either side of half-time as Bengaluru FC earned the bragging rights with a 2-0 triumph over southern rival Chennaiyin FC in an Indian Super League match at the Sree Kanteerava Stadium in Bengaluru on Saturday.

It was BFC's second straight win while CFC awaits its maiden victory of the season after the draw against FC Goa.

The fluid Bengaluru frontline proved too hot to handle for the Chennaiyin defence. The host could have opened the scoring around the 10-minute mark, but Perovic's header went straight to goalkeeper Mohammad Nawaz after he was picked out by Williams. Perovic, stationed on the right wing, sent a teasing delivery towards the left, from where Borja Martinez produced a shot at goal. While Nawaz saved the initial attempt, an alert Williams was in the right place to tap the ball into the net and pounce on the rebound.

BFC doubled the lead in the early stages of the second period. Nawaz and the rest of the CFC defence unit failed to deal with a Borja cross from the left and were duly punished by Perovic, who had an empty net to ripple. Substitute Sunil Chhetri could have put the icing on the cake with the late goal, but Nawaz delivered a sharp save.
