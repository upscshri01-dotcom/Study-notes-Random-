# Six States sign ₹15,624-crore pact; tenth water dispute set to end under PM Modi: Shah

The Centre will bear approximately 90% financial burden of the Kishau multipurpose project while remaining 10% will be borne by the participating States in accordance with a 1994 agreement.

Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Delhi and Rajasthan on Tuesday signed an agreement for the implementation of the 422 MW Kishau multipurpose project, to be built at an estimated cost of ₹15,624 crore. The project will come up on the Tons river along the Uttarakhand-Himachal Pradesh border.

In the presence of Union Home Minister Amit Shah and Union Jal Shakti Minister C.R. Patil, the Chief Ministers of six States: Yogi Adityanath of Uttar Pradesh, Pushkar Singh Dhami of Uttarakhand, Sukhvinder Singh Sukhu of Himachal Pradesh, Rajasthan’s Bhajan Lal Sharma, Rekha Gupta of Delhi and Nayab Singh Saini of Haryana signed the project agreement in New Delhi.

Terming the agreement as “historic”, Mr. Shah said that since Narendra Modi became the Prime Minister, this is the tenth water dispute that is now set to be resolved. The Centre will bear approximately 90% of the financial burden of the project, while the remaining 10% will be borne by the participating States in accordance with the 1994 agreement. A 232.6-metre high concrete gravity dam will be constructed on the Tons with a water storage capacity of 1,562 million cubic metres. It will irrigate 97,000 hectares of land and generate 1,476 million units of clean hydropower. The Union Home Minister said Delhi will be the biggest beneficiary of the project.

Mr. Sukhu said the Himachal government’s efforts have resulted in breaking the eight-year-old deadlock regarding bearing the financial cost of the project. “The previous State government of the Bharatiya Janata Party had agreed to give ₹800 crore as the State’s share, which our government refused in the interest of the State, keeping in view the limited resources,” he told media persons.

He said Himachal would suffer the greatest loss from the project, as it is the State’s population that will be displaced, and asking it to shoulder an additional financial burden would therefore be unfair. The Chief Minister added that the State’s contribution to the nation’s progress deserved suitable compensation.

The Delhi cabinet on Tuesday passed a resolution expressing gratitude to the Prime Minister, Home Minister and Union Jal Shakti Minister following the agreement. A statement issued by the Chief Minister’s Office said it would enable Delhi to receive 109.9 million cubic metres of water per year, providing significant support in meeting the national capital’s drinking water requirements.

# CBI books activist over ‘illegal foreign funds under FCRA’

The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) has booked Arunachal environment activist and lawyer Bhanu Tatak for allegedly receiving ₹20.94 lakh in foreign funds without registration or prior permission under the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act (FCRA). The agency alleged the money was used to “organise and sustain” protests against two hydropower projects in the State.

There was no immediate response available from Ms. Tatak to the allegations.

The FIR, registered on August 20 by the CBI’s Economic Offences-II branch in Delhi, names Ms. Tatak and unknown persons under sections 11(1), 17, 19 and 35 of the Act.

It is alleged that Ms. Tatak, legal adviser of the Siang Indigenous Farmers Forum and Coordinator of Dibang Resistance, maintained a savings bank account with the State Bank of India, Roing, and from 2022 to 2025, ₹20,94,088 was received in the account through forex transactions without obtaining requisite prior permission under the FCRA from the competent authority, the Ministry of Home Affairs, the FIR said.

The preliminary enquiry alleged that the funds were “being utilised to organise and sustain anti-dam protests” in Arunachal Pradesh against the Siang Upper Multipurpose Project and Dibang Multipurpose Hydropower Project. It said Ms. Tatak received the money from foreign entities.

“The purpose of the receipt of funds was undisclosed,” the enquiry said, adding that the received funds were predominantly for use by Ms. Tatak through several UPI transactions.

It further alleged that she had not filed income tax returns during the financial years 2017-18 to 2025-26 and therefore had not made any declaration regarding receipt of funds from the foreign entities or organisations.

# ‘Valid Indian ID’: picked up on suspicion of being Bangladeshi, man released after 3 months

Nearly three months after a 66-year-old West Bengal resident was picked up from his home in the middle of the night on suspicion of being a Bangladeshi national, he was released on bail on Tuesday.

Jalil Akhtar, a resident of Uttar Dinajpur district’s Bagroi village, was woken up from sleep and picked up by the police in the early hours of June 20. His family members alleged that the detention was illegal since he possesses all valid documents proving his citizenship.

On July 4, he was handed over to the Border Security Force along with three others to be “deported” to Bangladesh. However, Mr. Akhtar’s “deportation process” could not be completed despite two attempts while the three were successfully sent back.

During his incarceration, Mr. Akhtar was kept in two holding centres and then lodged is Islampur jail. In the entire duration, the police failed to prove that his documents were “fake”. When he was produced before the Additional Chief Judicial Magistrate (ACJM) of Islampur court on September 2, the investigating officer of the case gave a written declaration that the identity documents submitted by Mr. Akhtar were “valid”. The police, in its report, submitted that his voter ID, PAN card and his records in the 2002 SIR list were “genuine Indian documents”. He first got himself registered as a voter in 1995, according to the police.

Opposing his bail prayer, Assistant Public Prosecutor (APP) Sanjay Bhawal submitted in the court that Mr. Akhtar’s bail may pose a threat to during the security interests of the state. His counsels Syed Nafrul Islam and Firoz Ahmed argued that the investigators could not prove the documents relied upon by the accused are fabricated.

Directing the police to release him on bail, ACJM Jamshed Shaikh said, “The APP himself fairly submits that there is no single document which, in every case, can conclusively establish citizenship by birth.” The court also acknowledged that the victim holds valid documents proving his nationality.

# Busy reading, translating in jail, says former scribe booked under NSA over Noida stir

It has been five months since 64-year-old Shambhavi last met her husband Satyam Verma, a freelance translator and former journalist who was arrested on April 17 in connection with the Noida workers’ protest. “I could never have imagined that those in power could be afraid of people like Satyam,” she told The Hindu.

Uttar Pradesh police booked him in 11 cases in addition to charging him under the National Security Act (NSA) in connection with the agitation and sent him to the Greater Noida district jail.

Earlier this month, the Allahabad High Court had quashed the detention of the other individual — 25-year-old student Akriti Chaudhary — booked under the NSA over the Noida stir while court deferred the hearing on Mr. Verma’s bail plea after the State counsel sought an opportunity to file objections to his application, citing parity with Ms. Chaudhary. The court scheduled the next hearing for September 23.

In her detention order against the former journalist, where she invoked the NSA, Ms. Roopa stated that the former journalist had “engaged in criminal conspiracy to incite widespread violence and harm the state”.

‘Link’ to Nepal protests
It cited the police report, based on which the NSA was invoked, accusing him of “indirectly instigating the workers to indulge in violence” not only in Noida but “also in Nepal, leading to the ‘Gen Z’ revolution”. The police report describes Mr. Verma as an “ideological catalyst” and a Marxist who inspires “the new generation to join rebel organizations” and who engages in “violent writing work hidden under a false cover”.

“Satyam and I have shared a close association for nearly four decades. During this period, Satyam worked with reputed media organisations and later left his job to take up editing work. I was with him when he was editing the [Rajkamal Prakashan’s] World Classics series. I was also with Satyam when he translated Upton Sinclair’s The Jungle into Hindi,” said Ms. Shakambhari.

Speaking to The Hindu at the jail on a rainy afternoon, the freelance translator said he had lost significant weight since his incarceration. He said initially he had difficulty in procuring medicines for ailments such as diabetes. “Things are better now. I am taking my medicines and keeping busy with reading and translation work. We also play volleyball sometimes with a ball made of discarded polythene. That’s just how we pass time here,” he said.

Mr. Verma was picked up from Janchetna bookshelves in Lucknow on April 17, four days after the violence in Noida. “The police had been after us since he had put up a Facebook post in support of the workers’ movement,” said Katyayani Sinha, his colleague and relative.

She added, “He was called to a police station on April 9 and forced to delete the post. On April 13, the police again came to the Janchetna office in Lucknow and, on the pretext of questioning, took me, Satyam and Sanjay, our colleagues, to the Hasanganj police station and kept us there for the entire day.” Janchetna is a book distributor. Mr. Verma, a founding member, currently serves as its treasurer.

“On April 17, around 11.30 a.m., a large police contingent surrounded the office. They seized laptops, mobile phones, books and other items and took Satyam away. They also searched his home without a search warrant. They seized his personal belongings and took him to Noida without an arrest warrant,” said Ms. Sinha. His lawyers have disputed the legality of the arrest and alleged that due process had not been followed.

The NSA against him was invoked on May 12 while he was in jail. On May 26, he was produced before the NSA Advisory Board where the police submitted additional grounds for his arrest, which his counsel Manik Gupta claimed was in contravention of the law.

“Verma was unaware of the complete grounds of his arrest when he appeared before the board, which was illegal,” said Mr. Gupta.

He said, “As per the charges, the ‘conspiracy’ to instigate workers was hatched at Karawal Nagar on March 22. The Allahabad High Court has already noted in its order with respect to that event, that the gathering was nothing more than an innocuous inauguration of a library for the children of poor workers... The police have not been able to present any evidence so far to corroborate their claims.”

Perusing prison library
Mr. Verma said he has been busy reading books at the prison library, including D.N. Jha’s Ancient India: In Historical Outline, R.S. Sharma’s Early Medieval Indian Society: A Study in Feudalisation and The Making of the Madras Working Class by D. Veera-Raghavan. He has also been translating the works of Howard Fast and Bertolt Brecht in Hindi.
