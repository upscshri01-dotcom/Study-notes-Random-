# Govt. surrendering to U.S. pressure: Cong. on UPI fee

Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge and the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha, Rahul Gandhi, on Tuesday accused the Narendra Modi government of opening the door to charges on Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions, with Mr. Gandhi alleging that the move was the result of the government "surrendering to American pressure".

The government's "loot" had now reached UPI, Mr. Kharge said, while Mr. Gandhi alleged that a recent notification had quietly paved the way for imposing a Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) on merchant transactions above ₹2,000.

"No fees on UPI has been changed to 'no fees on UPI transactions up to ₹2,000'," Mr. Kharge said in a post on social media platform X, alleging that the government was planning to impose a "Digital Payments Tax" on the public when inflation had already squeezed household savings.

Mr. Kharge questioned the government's decision against the backdrop of the Centre's earlier emphasis on digital payments after demonetisation. He also referred to Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman's recent statement in Parliament that no tax or charge would be levied on UPI transactions, and asked whether reports of a possible ₹25 charge on a ₹5,000 transaction, and up to ₹350 on a ₹10,000 transaction, were correct.

"The government had provided that MDR could be levied on merchant UPI transactions above ₹2,000, Mr. Gandhi said. The government says customers will not be charged, he said, but asked where the cost imposed on merchants would ultimately come from.

"Added to the prices, straight out of the customer's pocket," Mr. Gandhi said.

American payment companies had long opposed India's zero-MDR policy, and the government had now opened the way for changing it, he alleged. "Just like with the U.S. trade deal, compromised PM Modi is once again surrendering to American pressure," Mr. Gandhi said.

Congress general secretary Jairam Ramesh said the notification under the amended Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 prohibited banks and payment system providers from charging for UPI transactions of up to ₹2,000 but did not provide explicit protection for transactions above that threshold.

"Decision Making in Mo-di Govt - Aug 6: Hon'ble Finance Minister says 'No decision has been taken on MDR'. Aug 10: FM in Parliament says 'No MDR framework has yet been final-ised'. Sep 14: Notification is issued. Within 24 hrs, MDR charges introduced." "Another example of Modi Govt's Fast moves (First Announce, Subsequently Think)," Mr. Ramesh said in a post on X.

His colleague Pawan Khera said the 0.4% MDR on UPI transactions above ₹2,000 should be called the "Modi Tax".

Fake news, says BJP
Responding to the criticism, the BJP on Tuesday accused the Congress of spreading "fake news" about UPI transaction charges, saying the government has made it clear that MDR charges will not be levied on consumers.

BJP spokesperson Pradeep Bhandari hit back at the Congress, calling the Opposition party a "jhooth ki dukan (shop of lies)".

"Congress is once again spreading fake news. 96% of UPI merchant (P2M) transactions are ₹2,000 or below, and they are 100% charge-free. RuPay debit card payments remain charge-free. Consumers pay zero transaction charge. P2P UPI transactions remain completely free," he said in a post on X. "The Government has made it absolutely clear: MDR charges will NOT be levied on consumers," the BJP leader added.

BJP leader Amit Malviya also termed the claims by Congress as "completely false".

# UCC should be brought through Parliament, not 'backdoor': Omar tells BJP

Questioning the move to use the "back-door entry", Jammu and Kashmir Chief Minister Omar Abdullah on Tuesday said the BJP should implement the Uniform Civil Code by legislating it in Parliament rather than pushing it through the BJP-ruled States.

"The government should not take the back-door route and should instead bring the Bill before Parliament. If you have numbers, bring it to Parliament. Why are you doing this through different States?" Mr. Abdullah said.

He also flagged that several National Democratic Alliance allies were "opposed to the idea of the UCC". "Not all the NDA allies are in the favour of the proposal. I don't think they will be able to pass this in Parliament. That is why they are taking the [BJP-ruled States] path," he said.

Stating that a measure of this nature "could not be considered universal if it was implemented only in BJP-ruled States", Mr. Abdullah said, "The whole country is not a BJP-ruled State. There are a number of States and territories that the BJP does not rule. Therefore, if you apply it only to BJP States, it's not universal."

He pitched that "such an issue should not be decided by individual States and must be addressed at the national level through Parliament".

Border de-escalation
On the de-escalation along the Line of Actual Control (LAC), Mr. Abdullah said it was a welcome step.

"Anything that reduces tension on the Line of Control or the Line of Actual Control is a good step. Particularly for those people who live close to it, and we know how difficult it is for people living near the LoC or LAC. Similarly, we hope, over time, that along the Line of Control also, things will get better," he said.

On China hailing India's foreign policy, Mr. Abdullah said New Delhi's foreign policy had always been independent and there was no need to seek validation from other countries.

"Our foreign policy is our own. Why do we look for other countries' certificates? The danger with something like this is that tomorrow we China to say something against our foreign policy, then what will you do? Therefore, I think we should not give too much importance to what other countries say about our foreign policy, whether positive or negative, because we can't cherry-pick. Tomorrow, if there is opposition, we will say, 'Don't interfere in our policy'," he said.

He said the BRICS Summit's condemnation of the Pahalgam terror attack was a success of India's foreign policy. "The fact that Pahalgam itself was condemned (at BRICS Summit) is a big thing. Please see how many allies of Pakistan were in that room. Therefore, even for Pahalgam to be mentioned by name, that in itself is a success of our foreign policy," he added.

# Will analyse and decide: JD(U) clarifies stance on UCC

The Janata Dal (United) clarified its stance on the Uniform Civil Code (UCC) on Tuesday, with a senior party leader stating that they will examine the details of the Bill before taking a final position.

"The JD(U) will first analyse and study the provisions of the UCC, and if they are found unexceptionable, why should we oppose it for no reason?" said senior JD(U) leader and Bihar Deputy Chief Minister Vijay Kumar Chaudhary.

Meanwhile, Opposition RJD legislator Bhai Virendra extended an open invitation to JD(U) chief Nitish Kumar to leave the alliance with BJP and rejoin hands with the RJD. "On the UCC, Nitish Kumar has already taken a stand. We will not let the UCC be implemented in Bihar. Come join hands with us again," the RJD leader said, asserting that the present government was working on instructions from Nagpur (where the RSS headquarters is located).

On party name change
When asked about party national working president Sanjay Kumar Jha's proposal to rename the JD(U) as Janata Dal (Nitish), Mr. Chaudhary said the issue would be discussed at the party's national executive and council meetings before a decision is taken.

# BJP fields mother of Suvendu’s slain aide

The Bharatiya Janata Party on Tuesday nominated Hasirani Rath as party candidate for Nandigram assembly byelection. Ms. Rath is the mother of Chandranath Rath, former personal assistant of West Bengal Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari who was killed on May 6.

The byelection is being held as Mr. Adhikari had relinquished the seat after getting elected from both Bhabanipur and Nandigram in the 2026 Assembly elections. The Mamata Banerjee-led camp of Trinamool has fielded Sanchita Pradhan (Dey).

# Rahul, Priyanka, and Venugopal hold review meetings amid reshuffle buzz

Ahead of a possible organisational reshuffle of All India Congress Committee (AICC) secretaries, senior party leaders Rahul Gandhi, K.C. Venugopal, and Priyanka Gandhi Vadra on Tuesday met several secretaries in batches to take stock of party issues.

Sources told The Hindu that Mr. Gandhi asked the secretaries about the problems they faced in the States they were handling, and whether they were receiving the support required to work effectively. He urged them to spend at least 15 days in their respective States.

The meetings focused on the nature of work being undertaken by the secretaries, their roles and responsibilities, and the time they were spending in the States assigned to them.

A recurring concern raised by some secretaries was the absence of clearly defined roles and responsibilities. Several of them felt that they had little say in the functioning of the organisation in the States, and were often dependent on States in-charge for assignments.

The discussions also touched upon the need for greater responsibility for the secretaries, and a system through which their feedback could be formally taken into account.

The secretaries are also expected to be assessed in the context of the Congress's organisational progress, including the programmes, like the Sangathan Srijan Abhiyan.

'Routine exercise'
One secretary described the meeting as a routine organisational exercise, saying such interactions were held from time to time to assess the work of those in the system, and identify areas for further strengthening of the party organisation.

# Key partners TDP, Shiv Sena support Shah’s statement on UCC in all NDA-ruled States

After Home Minister Amit Shah's assertion that a Uniform Civil Code will be implemented in all NDA-ruled States before 2029, key NDA allies TDP and Shiv Sena on Tuesday backed the BJP on the issue.

TDP's Andhra Pradesh president P. Srinivasa Rao said in Amaravati that it is bound by the Union Home Minister's statement on UCC as an NDA partner. "Yes, I have seen our Union Home Minister's statement on UCC and as NDA partners we are bound to go by his statement," Mr. Rao, who represents Gajuwaka Assembly Constituency, said.

"And critics, of course, some say that it (UCC) infringes on religious freedom. Our country is culturally diverse. But we always think that this UCC will strengthen and unify the country," he said.

Maharashtra Deputy Chief Minister and Shiv Sena president Eknath Shinde backed Mr. Shah on the UCC issue, saying Sena founder late Bal Thackeray had envisioned the common law for all.

"Hinduhridaysamrat Balasaheb Thackeray's vision was 'one country, one law' with equal justice for all. Union Home Minister Amit Shah has also stated the same position on UCC," Mr. Shinde said.

He added that the Mahayuti government in Maharashtra remains positive about the UCC and will make a definitive decision at an appropriate time.

# SC junks ex-Delhi Mayor’s plea on police ‘raid’

The Supreme Court did not entertain on Tuesday a petition filed by former Delhi Mayor Farhad Suri for registration of an FIR against police officers who conducted an alleged midnight raid at his residence in New Delhi, purportedly in search of Abhishek Upadhyay, an independent journalist who flagged the Ram Temple donations' misappropriations.

A three-judge bench headed by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant asked the petitioner to approach the jurisdictional police station for the appropriate remedy. "No doubt that the competent police authorities will act strictly in accordance to law," the bench ordered.
