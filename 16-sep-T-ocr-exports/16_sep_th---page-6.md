# L-G approves 427 ancillary posts for 53 fast-track courts

Lieutenant-Governor V.K. Saxena has approved the creation of 427 ancillary posts across various categories for the 53 newly created courts of the Delhi Higher Judicial Service, established for the setting up of fast track special courts in the Capital, Lok Niwas stated in a release. The Finance Department concurred with the proposal, subject to the condition that 243 vacant ancillary posts for the existing 415 courts be filled on priority before the new posts are operationalised, the release added.

# ‘Buildings unsafe’: residents in 145 NDMC flats asked to vacate

The New Delhi Municipal Council (NDMC) has asked residents of 145 Type-I quarters at Valmiki Sadan, primarily occupied by sanitation workers and civic staff, to vacate the premises after a structural assessment found eight residential blocks unsafe for habitation and beyond economical repair. Of the affected quarters, 81 are in four three-storey blocks built in 1965-66, while 64 are in four four-storey blocks constructed in 1985-86, according to a release issued by the NDMC on Tuesday.

# DTC drivers’ strike disrupts commute; protest to continue

A strike by drivers employed through private agencies for the Delhi Transport Corporation (DTC) disrupted bus services across the city on Tuesday, leaving commuters stranded. Buses did not leave several depots, including Burari, Nangloi, Rewla Khanpur, Nehru Place and Shadipur. Some buses were also stopped mid-route, forcing passengers to seek alternative transport. The drivers are demanding higher wages and medical and social security benefits. The DTC Karamchari Ekta Union backed the strike and said the protest would continue on Wednesday. The drivers are paid ₹862 a day for eight hours of work, according to the union.

# Govt. to build hostels for 10,000 students, expand facilities in colleges: CM

Chief Minister Arvind Kejriwal on Tuesday said the Delhi government has decided to build hostels to accommodate 10,000 students enrolled in colleges and universities across the city, with sites identified and meetings held with various departments to move the initiative forward.

In a video message on X, Mr. Kejriwal said ensuring safe, convenient and affordable accommodation was a priority for the government. “Hostel construction is already under way at some Delhi government universities and institutions, and the scope of the initiative would now be expanded in view of the growing need,” he added.

The announcement comes amid growing worries among outstation students regarding safe and affordable student housing following the September 6 Satya Niketan building collapse, which housed a PG facility. Several student groups and Opposition parties have called attention to the absence of hostel accommodation.

Sharing details of the expansion of housing facilities for students, Mr. Kejriwal said new construction is under way or has been proposed at several institutions, including Ambedkar University in Dheerpur, G.B. Pant Engineering College and Polytechnic, Indira Gandhi Hospital and Medical College, and Delhi Sports University.

The government’s responsibility would not end with the construction of hostels, the CM said, adding that it would also ensure that students’ safety, comfort and essential needs were met. “The government will also conduct a survey of colleges that have sufficient space for hostel construction. The process of constructing hostels will be expedited after suitable locations are identified,” he added.

Hostels would also be constructed under the public-private partnership (PPP) model, as this would help speed up construction while ensuring proper maintenance and the continued provision of better facilities for students, Mr. Kejriwal said.

The CM also announced that the government has decided to double the monthly incentive for ASHA workers from ₹3,000 to ₹6,000. The proposal was approved at a Cabinet meeting Mr. Kejriwal chaired earlier in the day. She said the increase in the monthly incentive was an important step towards recognising ASHA workers’ contribution and ensuring their financial security.

# BJP legislator booked under SC/ST Act for assault on party worker

The Delhi police on Tuesday registered an FIR against Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) Mundka MLA Gajendra Darall and others, five days after a BJP mandal chief, Sunil Kumar, accused the legislator of beating him and subjecting him to casteist slurs at a party office, a senior officer said.

The FIR, registered at the Kanjhawala police station, included provisions of the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, the Arms Act and other relevant sections, the officer said, adding that a counter-complaint from Mr. Darall has also been received and is being investigated.

According to the FIR, the alleged assault took place on September 10 when Mr. Darall called Kanjhawala mandal president Mr. Sunil, who hails from a Scheduled Caste community, to the party office in the evening on the pretext of discussing voter lists, and along with other workers, allegedly confined and thrashed him through the night with pipes, belts and sticks. Mr. Sunil also alleged that the assailants used casteist slurs against him, adding that he was allowed to leave the following morning but threatened not to approach the police. Several videos of Mr. Sunil, purportedly showing his bruises, have since gone viral.

Delhi BJP president Harsh Malhotra said the party has referred the matter to its disciplinary committee and further action would be taken based on its report. Meanwhile, Kondli MLA Kuldeep Kumar (AAP) said he accompanied Mr. Sunil to the DCP (Rohini) office at 10 a.m. on Tuesday and helped him get the case registered. “After nearly six hours of struggle, Sunil Kumar’s FIR was finally registered,” he wrote on X.

# Better housing takes centre stage across DUSU poll campaigns

The Satya Niketan building collapse has brought the shortage of hostels and safety concerns in paying guest (PG) accommodation into focus, with candidates making better housing a key manifesto promise for the upcoming Delhi University Students’ Union (DUSU) elections.

Student outfits have been releasing their manifestos since September 12, with accommodation emerging as a common thread. They have promised new hostels, safer and regulated PG accommodation, and mechanisms to address students’ housing concerns.

The Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad (ABVP), which won three of the four central panel posts in last year’s elections, has promised new hostels for DU colleges, separate hostels for women students in every college and a dedicated student housing helpline.

Like the ABVP, the National Students’ Union of India (NSUI) has promised stricter implementation of the Delhi Rent Control Act through regular inspections, holding landlords accountable and action against violations. It has also proposed a common DU portal listing verified PG accommodations, along with details of licences, rents and ratings, and a grievance redressal mechanism.

The All India Students’ Association (AISA) and the Students’ Federation of India (SFI), which are contesting the elections jointly, have pledged at least two hostels in every college. “It is one of our core demands. The first thing a student thinks about when they join DU is finding reasonable accommodation,” said Akshat Shikhar, vice-presidential candidate for the AISA-SFI joint panel.

The Association of Students for Alternative Politics (ASAP), the students’ wing of the Aam Aadmi Party, has called for the Centre to construct hostels for all students. “Our main demand is that the university should provide hostels. And in the meantime, it should bear the expenses of PGs,” said Eeshna Gupta, co-incharge of ASAP.

# AAP alleges cover-up in student’s death at PG; police deny allegation

The Aam Aadmi Party (AAP) on Tuesday accused the Delhi police of trying to cover up the “death of a girl” at a paying guest (PG) accommodation in south-west Delhi’s Satya Niketan. The police, however, denied the allegation, saying they had not received any such complaint.

In a post on X, AAP Delhi president Saurabh Bharadwaj alleged that area residents had told him that a female student died a few weeks ago after falling from a PG under suspicious circumstances. He accused the police of hushing up the matter instead of conducting a fair investigation.

“If one of our daughters fell from a PG and died, the police should provide information to establish the circumstances in which she fell,” he said.

When asked about the allegation, South West Delhi Deputy Commissioner of Police Amit Goel said the police had not received any complaint regarding the incident. “We have already shared all the details. There is nothing new or additional. The details shared by us are factual and correct,” Mr. Goel said.

AAP has repeatedly accused BJP leaders of running a ‘PG mafia’ in south-west Delhi’s Satya Niketan. AAP has repeatedly accused Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) MLA Anil Sharma and other party leaders of running a “PG mafia” in the area and alleged that authorities are shielding them. AAP also accused the BJP government and the civic body of “selective action” amid the ongoing crackdown on unsafe buildings and the sealing of fifth and higher floors.

The allegations come in the wake of the September 6 collapse of a five-storey building housing a PG accommodation in Satya Niketan, in which seven persons lost their lives. In another X post, Mr. Bharadwaj stated, “The MCD is functioning like a mafia — houses of those who don’t pay bribes are demolished to instil fear in people, while no action is taken against those who do pay bribes. This is directly evidenced by the illegal PG owned by BJP MLA Anil Sharma on Ring Road.”

Mr. Sharma, who represents R.K. Puram, has rejected the allegations and threatened a defamation suit against AAP.
